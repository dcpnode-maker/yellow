## 2. Mistaken check-in: what you need to decide

Example: a receptionist accidentally presses **Check in** for Guest A, but Guest A has not arrived.

That action currently affects important operational truth:

- The guest appears in-house.
- The room appears occupied.
- Housekeeping may stop preparing it.
- Charges and automations may start.

Yellow should allow **Correct mistaken check-in**:

- Return the reservation to `Due in`
- Release the accidental occupancy
- Restore the room’s previous operational condition
- Keep the original check-in and correction in history
- Require a reason and manager permission

### B. The guest actually occupied a room, but the room/time/person was wrong

Yellow must not pretend the stay never happened. It should:

- Preserve the original check-in
- Correct the room through a room move
- Correct permitted timestamps through an audited amendment
- Reverse incorrect charges through new accounting entries

- Same business day, no room occupancy and no charges: manager may use **Mistaken check-in correction**.
